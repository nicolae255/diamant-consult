Can you sell a rented property in Brussels?

Yes, a landlord can sell a flat or house with a tenant in it in Brussels, but the tenant may have a right of first refusal to buy it. This right has been in force since 6 January 2024 (be.brussels) and applies to sales started since that date.

You can therefore sell it "let" (the lease continues with the buyer) or sell it "vacant" if the tenant leaves. Either way, the procedure must be followed before you sign anything.

Does the tenant have a right of first refusal in Brussels?

The tenant has this right if the home is their main residence, they are registered at the address and the lease is long-term. According to be.brussels, it covers main-residence leases of 9 years or more, and short-term leases are excluded. The notaries add that it also covers leases deemed long-term by tacit renewal, as well as student leases and shared tenancies if the tenant is registered at the property.

Sales between members of the same family are not covered. Other exclusions exist: your notary checks your exact situation before the property goes on the market.

How do you notify the tenant of the sale?

The landlord must notify the tenant by registered letter with acknowledgement of receipt before concluding the sale. The letter states the intention to sell, the tenant's right of first refusal, the description of the property, the price and the conditions. It counts as a sale offer to the tenant, and the price and conditions cannot be negotiated.

  • 30 days: the tenant's time to decide whether to buy.
  • If they refuse or do not reply: the property is put on the market as normal.
  • 7 more days: if the property is later offered to another buyer at a better price or on better terms, the tenant gets 7 additional days.

Source: notaire.be · The tenant's right of first refusal (in French). The notary and the estate agent handling the sale must check that the tenant has been properly informed, and inform them themselves if not. The right also applies to a public auction.

What happens if the tenant's right is ignored?

The tenant can take legal action against the buyer to take their place, by reimbursing the price paid and the deed costs. According to notaire.be, this action can be brought up to one year after the sale, and the wronged buyer can then turn against the seller, notary or agent at fault (be.brussels). A sale you do not want to see challenged is worth doing by the book from the start.

What happens to the lease when the property is sold?

The lease continues: the buyer takes it over as it stands. According to notaire.be, for a 9-year lease with a certain date the new owner must respect 6 months' notice, and for a short lease (3 years maximum) 3 months' notice. In Brussels, with a lease without a certain date that has run for at least 6 months, the buyer can give 6 months' notice if they want to live in the property themselves, notified within 6 months of the notarial deed.

These rules come from the notaire.be page read here and may change: your notary confirms what applies to your lease. Registering the lease protects it and gives it a certain date, which clarifies the position for the buyer.

Should you sell let or vacant?

It depends on your goal: a let property mainly suits investors who want a tenant in place, while a vacant one also suits buyers who want to live there. A few pointers:

  • Selling let: the tenant stays and the rent continues, but viewings need their agreement. The owner can never enter without their consent (notaire.be).
  • Selling vacant: requires the tenant to leave, for example by mutual agreement, before the property goes on the market.

If you still let the property, also check that the rent respects the indicative rent grid, because an investor buyer will look at it. For the rest of the process, see selling property in Brussels in 2026 and how long it takes to sell.
