What happens to the house in a divorce or separation?

When a couple separates, the co-owners have three options for the house or apartment: sell it, have one partner buy out the other, or keep it together in joint ownership. That is what notaire.be describes (article of 5 January 2026). In Brussels, selling is usually the simplest route when neither partner can or wants to buy the other out.

  • Sell: the sale price first repays the mortgage, then what is left is shared according to each partner's ownership rights.
  • Buy out the other's share: one partner keeps the home and pays the other a balancing payment (soulte), based on the property's current value.
  • Stay in joint ownership: both remain owners, for example when they run a business together in the same building.

Can one spouse sell the family home alone?

No, not if you are married or legal cohabitants. According to notaire.be, the family home is protected: when it belongs to only one spouse, it cannot be sold or mortgaged without the other's consent. For married couples, both must therefore agree to sell.

For legal cohabitants, the owner or tenant cannot simply make the other leave either. For de facto cohabitants, the owner can generally decide to sell, and the other partner has to go to court if they disagree. Your exact position depends on your status and marital property regime: your notary will confirm it.

What happens to the mortgage?

Until the sale deed is signed, each ex-partner remains jointly liable for repaying the mortgage, says notaire.be. If one of you keeps the home, that person becomes the sole borrower: they take over the capital and interest repayments, and the bank must agree.

In a sale, the bank is repaid from the price. This matters when setting the price: you need to know how much capital is still owed before choosing a realistic asking price.

How much does buying out the other's share cost in Brussels?

In Brussels, as in Wallonia, registration duties on a buy-out (the "droits de partage") are 1% of the property value stated in the deed, according to notaire.be. If the real sale value is higher, the duties are calculated on that higher value. In Flanders the 1% rate only applies to married couples and legal cohabitants (the latter with at least one year of cohabitation).

The difficulty is often the valuation: each side can appoint an expert, and opinions differ. Unequal contributions (own funds, works paid by one partner) are taken into account in the calculation.

How do you organise a sale when you are separating?

  1. Consult a notary to confirm your status, your rights and the outstanding mortgage.
  2. Choose the asking price together. On a family home, an over-ambitious price prolongs an already difficult period: see how long it takes to sell in Brussels.
  3. Prepare the file: EPC certificate, soil certificate, urban planning information.
  4. Agree who handles viewings and how both co-owners approve offers: the notary drafts the preliminary sale agreement once both have accepted an offer.
  5. Sign the deed at the notary's office: the notary carries out the administrative searches, reads the deed to the parties and completes the formalities.
