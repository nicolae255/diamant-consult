Selling in Brussels to buy elsewhere:
in which order, with what financing?

According to the notaries, more than four in ten Brussels residents buy their property outside the capital. If you sell in Brussels to buy in Flanders or Wallonia, here is how to organise both transactions.

Key points

  • In 2025, 43.6% of Brussels residents who bought a property did so outside Brussels, up from 39.9% in 2024 (notaire.be, 29 October 2026).
  • For houses, only 32% of Brussels buyers stay in Brussels (43% choose Flanders, 25% Wallonia); for apartments, 86% stay.
  • An average house cost about €583,000 in Brussels in 2025, against about €380,000 in Flanders and €270,000 in Wallonia.
  • A bridge loan covers the gap between buying and selling, but its conditions can be stricter and more expensive than a standard loan.

How many Brussels residents buy their next home outside Brussels?

In 2025, 43.6% of Brussels residents who bought a property did so outside the 19 communes, according to the notaries' federation, published on notaire.be on 29 October 2026. The share was 39.9% in 2024, 42.5% in 2023 and 44.7% in 2021. The trend is mostly about houses: only 32% of Brussels residents buying a house stay in Brussels, while 43% choose Flanders and 25% Wallonia. For apartments, 86% stay in the capital.

If you own a home in Brussels and dream of more space or greenery, you are not alone. Among Brussels couples buying a house, 45% chose Flanders in 2025. Buyers aged 31 to 35 are the most likely to cross the regional border: 26% of Brussels residents who bought a house outside Brussels were in that age group.

Why sell in Brussels to buy elsewhere?

Price comes first: according to notaire.be, an average house cost about €583,000 in Brussels in 2025, against about €380,000 in Flanders and €270,000 in Wallonia. The notaries also point to the need for space for a growing family, the wish for greenery and, in Wallonia, the reduced registration duties for first-time buyers introduced in 2025.

For a Brussels owner, the equation is simple: the sale price of your property largely finances what comes next. To see where the Brussels market stands before you decide, read our analysis of the Brussels property market in 2026.

Should you sell first or buy first?

There is no single answer, but the order depends on three things: how much you can borrow, how the market treats your property and how long a gap you can live with. These are the two usual scenarios.

  • Sell first: you know your exact budget before committing, but you may need temporary housing between the sale and the purchase.
  • Buy first: you avoid a temporary move, but you carry two properties at once, with the risk of paying two loans if the sale takes longer than planned.

Selling time matters a lot here: see how long it takes to sell a property in Brussels.

How do you finance the gap between buying and selling?

Your bank may offer a bridge loan (crédit-pont), which covers the period between buying the new property and selling the old one. According to notaire.be, the bank advances the amount of your current home's sale, you repay it from the sale price, and you pay interest in the meantime. Conditions can be stricter and more expensive than a standard loan, and it is added to your existing credit.

Another option mentioned by the notaries: keep your current loan and transfer the mortgage registration to the new property, which may avoid early repayment penalties and new file fees. Ask your bank about these options before signing a purchase agreement.

Can you sell on condition that you buy another property?

Yes, the sale agreement (compromis) can include a suspensive condition. According to notaire.be, the compromis binds seller and buyer and can carry suspensive or resolutive conditions. A suspensive condition tied to obtaining a loan protects the buyer; a seller can also provide for first being able to buy another home. Such a clause is drafted with your notary: in practice, the broader it is, the less attractive your property looks to buyers, because it delays certainty that the sale will go through.

Frequently asked questions

Should I sell before buying my next home?

It depends on how much you can borrow and how long a gap you can live with. Selling first gives you a certain budget; buying first avoids a temporary move but means carrying two properties at once.

What is a bridge loan?

According to notaire.be, it is a loan covering the period between buying a new property and selling the old one: the bank advances the sale amount and you repay it from the sale price, paying interest in the meantime.

Can I add a purchase condition to the sale agreement?

The compromis can contain suspensive conditions. A seller can provide for first being able to buy another home, but such a clause is drafted with the notary and may make the property less attractive.

Why do so many Brussels residents buy outside Brussels?

According to the notaries, price is the main factor (about €583,000 for a house in Brussels against about €380,000 in Flanders), along with space, greenery and, in Wallonia, reduced registration duties for first-time buyers.
