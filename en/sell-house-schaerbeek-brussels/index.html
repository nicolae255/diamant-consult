Selling in Schaerbeek:
the keys to success.

Market specifics, buyer expectations, and property types that move fastest in Schaerbeek.

Key points

  • Schaerbeek attracts young couples, families and investors thanks to its proximity to the European Quarter and prices that are more affordable than in Ixelles or Etterbeek.
  • Renovated townhouses (Avenue Louis Bertrand, Josaphat Park), well-located two-bedroom apartments and converted workshops sell fastest.
  • Buyers scrutinise the EPC, the roof and window frames, planning compliance and, for apartments, the service charges and reserve fund.

Schaerbeek: a neighbourhood in full transformation

Schaerbeek attracts a wide range of buyers: young couples, families, investors. Its proximity to the European Quarter and its grand art deco townhouses are among its strengths. The market is active, but demanding on presentation and pricing.

The property types that sell fastest

In Schaerbeek, three types of property move particularly quickly:

  • Renovated townhouses (especially around Avenue Louis Bertrand and Josaphat Park): highly sought after by Brussels families and expats.
  • Well-positioned two-bedroom apartments: popular with first-time buyers and investors.
  • Converted workshops and commercial units: Schaerbeek's mixed-use fabric is generating growing interest in transformation projects. These properties sell quickly when planning permissions are in order.

What buyers look at first

Schaerbeek buyers are often well-informed. They know market prices, have visited other properties, and arrive with specific questions. What they scrutinise:

  • The EPC: an E or F rating is a systematic objection. Plan either targeted insulation works or factor the cost into your price.
  • Roof and window condition: many Schaerbeek houses are old. A recent roof report reassures buyers.
  • Planning compliance: works carried out without a permit block the sale or trigger tough negotiations.
  • Service charges for apartments: a well-managed building with a healthy reserve fund is a strong selling point.

Pricing strategy in Schaerbeek

The value of a property in Schaerbeek depends on the street, its condition, the EPC and recent comparable sales: that is the only reliable basis for setting a price. For context, the notaries’ barometer puts the average house price in Brussels at €609,227 over the first nine months of 2026. Properties needing renovation must build the estimated cost of works into the price — buyers will do so anyway.

A listing with a structured viewing window (two weekends, a deadline for offers) typically generates more simultaneous offers, which gives the seller a stronger negotiating position.
