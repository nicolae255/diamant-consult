Selling in Brabant Wallon:
what changes vs Brussels.

Timelines, taxation, buyer expectations, what you need to know when selling a property in Wallonia.

Key points

  • Since 1 January 2025, registration duties in Wallonia are 3% for a main residence (12.5% for a second home or investment); Brussels remains at 12.5% with an allowance for first-time buyers.
  • The Walloon seller's file (planning information, soil certificate, Walloon EPC, co-ownership documents) often takes longer than in Brussels: start early.
  • Buyers in Walloon Brabant are often families moving out of Brussels, who pay close attention to schools, train links and the condition of the property.

Brabant Wallon: a market in its own right

Brabant Wallon (BW) is one of Belgium's most dynamic provinces in terms of real estate. Its proximity to Brussels, perceived quality of life, reputable schools and green surroundings make it a sought-after destination for Brussels families looking for space. Municipalities like Waterloo, La Hulpe, Rixensart, Genval and Wavre show sustained demand and some of the highest prices in Wallonia.

If you own a property in this area and are considering selling, several aspects differ significantly from the Brussels market.

Taxation: registration duties, 2025 reform

This is the most tangible difference for buyers and therefore indirectly for you as the seller. A major reform came into effect on 1 January 2025 in Wallonia.

  • In Wallonia: since 1 January 2025, registration duties are 3% (instead of 12.5%) for the purchase of a sole own home (wallonie.be). The 12.5% rate still applies in other cases (second home, investment).
  • In Brussels: the rate remains 12.5%, but the first €200,000 is exempt if the price does not exceed €600,000, the buyer owns no other home and moves in within 3 years for at least 5 years (notaire.be).

What this means for sellers: at 3%, buying a sole own home costs far less in registration duties. On a €400,000 property, duties fall from €50,000 (12.5%) to €12,000 (3%), excluding notary fees. Duties are less of a brake, which can make negotiating the price itself easier.

EPC in Wallonia: specific rules

In Wallonia, the EPC regulatory framework differs slightly from Brussels. The Walloon EPC certificate is issued by an assessor accredited by the Walloon Region (not the Brussels Region). It is mandatory before any listing for sale or rent, and must appear in the listing from the moment of publication.

Check that your EPC certificate is still valid with an approved assessor before listing.

Wallonia-specific documents

The sale file in Wallonia includes elements that differ from Brussels:

  • Planning information: request it from the municipality well in advance.
  • Soil information: to be obtained before the sale. Essential.
  • Mortgage information: requested from the relevant office.
  • For co-owned properties: minutes of the last 3 general meetings, charge statements, co-ownership rules.

Walloon administrative timelines are often longer than in Brussels. Start gathering documents as soon as the decision to sell is made.

Buyer profile in Brabant Wallon

Buyers in Brabant Wallon have a distinct profile:

  • Often families with children leaving Brussels for more space.
  • Average budget higher than in other Walloon provinces.
  • Very attentive to local schools, transport links (train to Brussels) and nearby amenities.
  • Demanding on overall condition, they compare with well-renovated Brussels properties.
  • Often first-time buyers or moving up, financing with a mortgage.

Sale timelines: plan wider

Administrative delays, especially for planning information, can slow down the finalisation of the sale agreement. Plan ahead and communicate clearly with buyers about it.

Frequently asked questions

What are registration duties in Wallonia in 2026?

Since 1 January 2025, registration duties in Wallonia are 3% for the purchase of a sole own home, instead of 12.5%. The 12.5% rate still applies to second homes and investment properties.

Does it take longer to sell in Walloon Brabant than in Brussels?

Administrative delays, especially for planning information, can lengthen the preparation of the file and the completion of the deed. Start gathering documents as soon as you decide to sell.

Which specific documents do you need to sell in Wallonia?

In Wallonia, the seller's file includes planning information, soil information, the Walloon EPC and, for co-ownerships, the minutes of the last 3 general meetings, the service charge statements and the co-ownership rules.
