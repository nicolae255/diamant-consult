Selling property
in Brussels in 2026.

Preparation, valuation, viewing strategy: the essentials for maximising your sale price in a changing Brussels market.

Key points

  • To sell well in Brussels in 2026, the asking price must be realistic: an overpriced property stays online, gets viewings without offers and ends up selling for less.
  • A serious valuation compares recent sales (less than 6 months) in the neighbourhood and accounts for the EPC, condition, floor and orientation.
  • Grouped viewings over three weekends, with a deadline for offers, create healthy competition between buyers.
  • The Brussels seller's file (EPC, soil certificate, planning information, title deed, co-ownership documents) must be ready before listing.

The Brussels market in 2026: what has changed

According to the notaries’ barometer published in October 2026, the average house price in Brussels reached €609,227 (+4.8%) over the first nine months of 2026, with a stable number of sales. Credit has become more expensive, though: buyers are there, but they compare more. A sloppy listing is costly. Read our 2026 market analysis →

Every Brussels neighbourhood has its own dynamic, and the condition of the property and its EPC weigh heavily in buyers' decisions. That is why a valuation must start from comparable sales in your neighbourhood, not from an average.

Step 1: a realistic valuation, not an optimistic one

The most costly mistake a seller can make is overpricing. An overpriced property lingers online, accumulates viewings without offers, and eventually sells for less than a correctly priced listing would have from day one. Buyers check price histories on Immoweb.

To value correctly: compare recent sales (within six months) on your street or in your neighbourhood, factor in the EPC rating, overall condition, floor level and aspect. At Diamant Consult, we set this price with you at a free first meeting, based on a documented analysis.

Step 2: prepare the property before the photos

Photos are the first impression and often the last. A poorly presented property attracts fewer viewings, which leads to fewer offers and downward pressure on price.

  • Declutter every room: put things away, donate, store elsewhere.
  • Neutralise bold colours if possible (a coat of white paint can be enough).
  • Fix small visible defects: dripping tap, broken handle, loose seal.
  • Attend to the exterior: a clean facade and a trimmed garden make an immediate impression.

Step 3: a structured viewing strategy

Running an open-ended stream of viewings, one candidate after another, with no deadline, is a mistake. The better approach: set a defined viewing window (three weekends), announce a deadline for offers, and create healthy competitive pressure among candidates.

During viewings, avoid being present. Buyers speak more freely, picture themselves in the space more easily, and ask questions of the agent that they would never raise in front of the owner.

Step 4: documents to prepare in advance

In the Brussels Region, the seller's file is mandatory and takes time to assemble. Plan ahead:

  • EPC certificate (commission it as soon as the decision to sell is made)
  • Soil attestation
  • Urban planning information
  • For apartments: minutes of the last 3 owners' meetings, service charge statements, co-ownership regulations
  • Title deed (locate it or request it from your notary)

Without these documents, signing the preliminary agreement can be blocked or delayed, which cools buyer interest and can derail the sale entirely.

Frequently asked questions

What documents do you need to sell in Brussels?

In the Brussels Region you need the EPC certificate, the soil certificate, planning information and the title deed, plus, for apartments, the minutes of the last 3 general meetings, the service charge statements and the co-ownership rules.

Should you be present during viewings when selling your property?

No, it is better not to be. Buyers speak more freely, picture themselves more easily in the property and ask the agent questions they would not dare ask in front of the owner.

Why not overprice your property to leave room for negotiation?

An overpriced property stays online too long, gets viewings without offers and ends up selling for less than if it had been priced correctly from the start. Buyers check price histories on Immoweb.
